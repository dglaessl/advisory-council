## Securities framing (facts only)
- **506(c):** allows general solicitation; all purchasers must be accredited and the issuer must take reasonable steps to verify; Form D is filed after first sale; securities are restricted [14]. The deck's disclaimer describes this accurately. Form D is due within 15 calendar days of first sale [15].
- **Form D "related persons" (Item 3):** each executive officer, director and promoter (promoter looks back 5 years) of the issuer. Job title alone does not decide it. Item 12 requires anyone paid sales compensation (finders included) with CRD numbers. The issuer certifies it is not disqualified under 506(d) [15]. Item 6 selects the exemption claimed.
- **Bad-actor rule (506(d)):** covered persons: issuer; directors, general partners, managing members; executive officers and officers participating in the offering; 20% beneficial owners; promoters; fund investment managers and principals; paid solicitors and their officers. Disqualifying events include securities-related criminal convictions, injunctions, certain regulator orders, SEC cease-and-desist orders (scienter-based fraud), and SRO suspensions. Look-backs are 5 or 10 years, or while a bar is in effect. Events before 2013-09-23 need disclosure to purchasers [16]. Secondary source, from 2019; the rule also has a reasonable-care exception and waivers (from my background knowledge, not fetched; verify in 17 CFR 230.506(d)-(e)).
- **Canada NI 45-106:** s.2.3 accredited-investor exemption (signed risk acknowledgement for individuals, not applicable in Ontario under that section); s.2.9 offering-memorandum exemption (OM with misrepresentation certificate signed by officers, directors and promoters, signed risk acknowledgement, two-day cancellation right, OM filed within 10 days, per-investor caps in several provinces for non-accredited) [17]. Dealer registration is separate: an exemption that refers to a registered dealer applies only where the dealer is registered for that trade [17]. Source is a 2016 consolidation; check the current text. The deck's Canadian "club deals" name no exemption.

## Bay Area by-right policy (relevance to the strategy)
- **SB 423 (2023):** extends SB 35 streamlining, removes the fixed sunset (one source; other summaries say 2036, unresolved), reaches the coastal zone under the Coastal Act, adds labor protections [18].
- **SB 684 (eff. 2024-07-01) / SB 1123 (eff. 2025-07-01):** ministerial subdivision and up to 10 units; multifamily lots up to 5 acres; SB 1123 adds vacant single-family lots up to 1.5 acres; 60-day decision, else deemed approved; excluded hazard areas and recent tenant-occupied sites [19]. Capped at 10 units, so it fits only the bottom of the deck's "10-80 homes" range.
- **AB 2011 (eff. 2023-07-01, sunset 2033):** by-right for 5+ units on commercially zoned urban infill, but with affordability set-asides (e.g., 15% lower-income for mixed-income rentals), prevailing-wage and skilled-and-trained labor, and apprenticeship plus health spending at 50+ units; 60 or 90 day decisions [20][21]. These conditions bear on a "by-right, market-rate" yield case.

## Red flags
- 517 W Main is a Neutral project and the deck's US partner shares the Neutral CEO's name (Nathan/Nate Helbach). Daniel sits on Neutral's board (from memory). Related-party and independence question for the council; the deck does not name Neutral.
- Deck is dated Nov 2026 but cites Q1 2026 data; two of nine claims are unsupported and one (vacancy) is contradicted by CoStar's forecast.
- Scouts or the deck contain no instructions to follow; none were encountered.

Confidence: medium. Main uncertainty: the paywalled CoStar series, and the missing primary legal text.
